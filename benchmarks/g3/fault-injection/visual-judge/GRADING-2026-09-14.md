## The observation that drove each detection

**`wrong-proportions`.** Spec: a rail shim 100 × 20 × 3 mm, aspect 5:1. The render shows a broad
plate near 5:3. *Advisory, not blocking:* the holes render as raised discs — a flat-lit shading
artifact, not a geometry claim.

**`feature-on-wrong-face`.** Spec puts the rib on the pad's long centreline. The render shows it
running across the short direction and well off-centre, dividing the pad into a narrow strip and a
wide one. The rib's own proportions are correct; only its host edge is wrong.

**`wrong-dimension-callout` — the most informative case in the suite.** The sheet reads
`hole_pitch = 60.00`; the spec states 80 mm (100 mm long, 10 mm inset per end). Every other callout
is correct (100.00, 20.00, 3.00, 6.00). Critically the **part is correct and the drawn geometry
proves it** — the TOP view's holes sit at roughly 10 % and 90 % of the 100 mm length, an 80 mm
pitch. A judge that re-derived the pitch from the view would measure 80, find it self-consistent,
and pass. Only reading the callout *as printed* and comparing against the **spec text** catches it.
No geometry assertion can reach this fault.

**`mirrored-part`.** Both brackets render in the same orientation, so the comparison isolates one
feature: the control's 9 mm hole sits near the **left** edge of the vertical leg, the planted part's
near the **right**. The spec fixes this — "12 mm in from the left-hand edge when the bracket is
viewed with the horizontal leg pointing towards you" — so the mirror violates a stated requirement.
`expect_volume`, `expect_bbox`, `count_cylindrical_holes`, `hole_diameter` and `hole_through` are all
invariant under the reflection, so the geometry leg is blind to it by construction.

**`missing-flange`.** A plain sleeve, uniform outer diameter top to bottom. The spec calls for a
30 mm flange at one end; the part has none.

## Why the controls were cleared

**`control-correct-part`** renders as the specified L-bracket, hole against the left edge.

**`control-cosmetic-render-difference`** is the same ribbed pad under a different camera and a warm
tone. Geometry matches the spec; per `RUBRIC.md` camera azimuth and material tone are explicitly
non-failures. Cleared.

**`control-symmetric-part-either-hand`** is a flat annular washer — rotationally symmetric, so
identical to its own mirror. Per the rubric a mirror on a symmetric spec is not a defect. This is the
control that makes `mirrored-part` meaningful: flagging one and clearing the other is only coherent
because the bracket's spec fixes a hand and the washer's does not.

## Two fixture defects found by grading, both since fixed

Recorded because they are the substance of what this exercise produced, and both passed every
automated check in the suite that built them.

**DW-127 — `mirrored-part` planted no detectable defect.** The original L-bracket was **achiral**:
its spec fixed no handedness and the "mirror" equalled a 180° rotation, so the planted part and the
correct part were the same physical object. Flagging it would have required also flagging the
symmetric control — the suite was unpassable by construction. Fixed by adding a chiral feature (the
9 mm hole) and amending the spec to name the edge, which is an AD-19-sensitive change recorded in
DW-129.

**DW-128 — the cosmetic control rendered illegibly.** `_COSMETIC_ORIENTATION` produced a near
edge-on view with z-fighting where the pad's proportions could not be read. Graded CANNOT ASSESS,
which on a clean control counts against the false-alarm bar. Fixed by choosing an orientation that
keeps all three principal faces readable — and verifying it by looking, not by trusting the
quaternion.

## Independence caveat

AD-13 requires the judge not be the actor. This judge also drove the designer loop, so this is **not
the independent judge AD-13 specifies**. Exposure is bounded — the parts were authored before any
judge existed, and grading ran against a rubric written before any image was seen — but a fully
independent number needs a separate model or a separate context. The score below is recorded with
this caveat attached, not as a clean AD-13 measurement.

## What this does and does not support

**Does:** the visual leg is not vacuous. Five planted defects were caught by looking, including the
callout-versus-spec case no geometry assertion can reach, and two fixture defects were found that
every automated check had passed over.

**Does not:** clear the AD-13 bars with confidence (CI lower bound 0.566), substitute for the
in-loop judge (which still needs a programmatic client and a credential, per decision 017), or
constitute an independent measurement.
